## What is a stock screener?

A stock screener filters thousands of stocks based on criteria you define — market cap, P/E ratio, dividend yield, revenue growth, sector, and more. Instead of scrolling through endless lists, you set your parameters and let the screener surface the stocks that match.

For individual investors, a good screener replaces hours of manual research. For analysts and portfolio managers, it is the first step in any systematic stock selection process.

## What to look for in a free stock screener

Not all screeners are equal. Before picking one, consider these factors:

-   **Number of filters** — More filters mean more precise results. Basic screeners offer 10-15 filters; advanced ones offer 50+.
-   **Data freshness** — Some free screeners delay data by 15-20 minutes. Real-time data matters for active traders.
-   **Export options** — Can you download results as CSV or Excel? This is critical for further analysis.
-   **Market coverage** — US stocks only, or international markets too?
-   **Fundamental vs. technical** — Some screeners focus on fundamentals (P/E, revenue), others on technical indicators (RSI, moving averages), and a few cover both.

## 3\. TradingView Screener — best for technical analysis

TradingView combines charting and screening in one platform. The screener supports both fundamental and technical filters, and you can switch to a chart with one click.

### Strengths

-   Deep technical indicator library (RSI, MACD, Bollinger Bands, custom indicators)
-   Global market coverage — stocks, ETFs, forex, crypto

### Limitations

-   Free tier limits saved screeners and alerts
-   Export functionality requires a paid plan

## 4\. Investing.com Screener — best for international stocks

Investing.com covers markets worldwide. If you screen stocks outside the US — European, Asian, or emerging markets — this is one of the most comprehensive free options.

### Strengths

-   Covers 40+ global stock exchanges
-   Good selection of fundamental filters
-   Free to use with no account
-   Sector and industry classification for international markets

### Limitations

-   Ad-heavy interface
-   No direct CSV export in the free version
-   Technical filters are limited compared to TradingView

## 5\. Zacks Stock Screener — best for earnings-focused investors

Zacks is known for its earnings estimates and ranking system. The free screener lets you filter stocks by the Zacks Rank, earnings surprise history, and estimate revisions.

### Strengths

-   Unique Zacks Rank filter based on earnings estimate revisions
-   Earnings surprise and estimate data built in
-   Research reports available for screened stocks

### Limitations

-   Many advanced filters are locked behind Zacks Premium
-   US stocks only
-   Interface feels dated compared to Finviz or TradingView

## Tips for effective stock screening

### Start broad, then narrow

Begin with 2-3 high-level filters (market cap, sector, exchange) and progressively add more specific criteria. Starting too narrow risks filtering out good candidates.

### Combine fundamental and technical filters

A stock that looks cheap on fundamentals but has terrible price momentum may stay cheap for a long time. Combining both filter types gives you a more complete picture.

### Verify with actual financial data

Screener data is summary-level. Before making investment decisions, pull the full financial statements and examine the numbers in detail. For the price side, [FinGrab](https://fingrab.app/) exports the full history from Yahoo Finance directly to CSV.

### Save and revisit your screens

Markets change daily. A screen that returns zero results today might surface interesting stocks next month. Save your filter criteria and run the screen regularly.
